Thomas Edison

I am the inventor of the light bulb.

Home

Thomas Alva Edison (1847–1931) was one of the most prolific inventors in human history, holding a staggering 1,093 US patents. He was not only known for inventing single devices, but also turned out to have invented the modern industrial resource laboratory, which turned inventing into a collaborative, mass-production process. He made other things too, such as:

  • The Phonograph (1877): His first major breakthrough recorded sound onto tin-foil cylinders. It was the first time in human history that a machine could record and play back the human voice.
  • Motion Pictures: Edison's lab built the Kinetoscope (a peephole film viewer) and the Kinetograph (the movie camera). He also established "Black Maria," the world's very first movie production studio.
  • The Telephone: He significantly improved Alexander Graham Bell’s original telephone by developing the carbon button transmitter. This amplified the voice signal, allowing people to speak across much longer distances.
